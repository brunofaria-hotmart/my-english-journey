📚 Grammar I’m Learning

Use this as the main grammar reference. Focus on the meaning first, then the structure.

1. Present Simple

I / you / we / they → base verb · he / she / it → verb + s

Use: routines, habits, facts and regular situations.

I work from home. → Eu trabalho de casa.
She works here. → Ela trabalha aqui.
Do you speak English? / Does he work here?
I don't speak French. / She doesn't like coffee.
Important: after does/doesn't, the main verb returns to the base form: Does she like coffee? ❌ Does she likes coffee?

2. Present Continuous

am / is / are + verb-ing

Use: something happening now or a temporary situation.

I am studying English. / We are working on a new feature.
Are you working now? / I'm not working now.

3. Past Simple

past verb · questions/negatives → did + base verb

Use: a finished action in the past, especially with a finished time.

I finished the task yesterday. / We deployed it last Friday.
Did you finish the task? / I didn't finish the task.
Rule: did/didn't carries the past: didn't go, not didn't went.
Do you work here? → present. Did you work yesterday? → past. Did you do your homework? → did = helper, do = main verb.

4. Past Continuous

was / were + verb-ing

Use: an action that was in progress at a specific past moment, often interrupted by another action.

I was working at 8 PM.
I was studying when he called.
We were deploying when the pipeline failed.

5. Present Perfect

have / has + past participle

Use: a past action connected to now, a life experience, or a recent result.

I've finished my work. → Terminei meu trabalho / agora está terminado.
It has been deployed to production.
I've already finished. / Have you finished yet? / I haven't finished yet.
Important: ❌ I've finished it yesterday. → ✅ I finished it yesterday. A finished specific time normally uses Simple Past.

6. Present Perfect Continuous

have / has + been + verb-ing

Use: an activity that started in the past and continues until now, or recently stopped with current relevance. It emphasizes activity or duration.

I've been studying English for two days.
I've been working all morning.
for = duration → for two days. since = starting point → since Monday.
Compare: I've studied English for three months = fact/experience. I've been studying English for three months = activity/duration.

7. Future

will + base verb
Prediction: I think it will rain tomorrow.
Decision now: I'll help you. / Promise: I'll let you know.
am / is / are going to + base verb
Plan: I'm going to study English tonight.
Evidence: Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.
am / is / are + verb-ing
Fixed arrangement: I'm meeting my uncle tomorrow.

8. Modal Verbs

modal + base verb
ModalMeaningExample
canability / permission / possibilityI can help.
couldpast ability / polite request / possibilityCould you help me?
shouldadvice / expectationYou should practice.
wouldhypothetical / polite preferenceI would go if I had time.
mightpossibilityI might go later.
muststrong obligation / deductionHe must be tired.
Could is sometimes past ability: I could swim when I was five. But Could you...? is a polite request, not past tense.

10. Used to / Be used to / Get used to

StructureMeaningExample
used to + verbcostumava / fazia no passadoI used to live in Brazil.
be used to + noun/-ingestar acostumadoI'm used to working from home.
get used to + noun/-ingse acostumarI'm getting used to living in Canada.
Key difference: get used to = process. be used to = state.

11. This / These / That / Those

WordMeaningUseExample
thiseste/esta/issosingular + near/currentThis is my laptop.
theseestes/estasplural + near/currentThese are my keys.
thataquele/aquela/issosingular + far/previously mentionedThat was a good idea.
thoseaqueles/aquelasplural + far/previously mentionedThose are my bags.
Conceptual distance: “We changed the architecture yesterday. That caused another problem.” Here “that” refers to something previously mentioned, not physically far away.

12. TO / IN / ON / AT

WordCore ideaExamples
TOdirection / destination / addition / connectiongo to Canada · add monitoring to the YAML · talk to me
INinside / withinin the room · in the YAML · in the application
ONsurface / platformon the table · on GitHub · on AWS
ATpoint / specific placeat home · at the office · at the airport
Memory trick: TO → direction. IN → inside. ON → surface/platform. AT → point.
Technical examples: add Sentry to the project · configure monitoring in the production YAML · check the logs on New Relic · meet me at the office.

13. Already / Yet

I've already finished. → Já terminei.
Have you finished yet? → Já terminou?
I haven't finished yet. → Ainda não terminei.

14. Though

It's expensive. I like it, though. → É caro. Mas eu gosto.
Though it was raining, we went outside. → Embora estivesse chovendo, saímos.
Even though I'm tired, I want to go. → Mesmo estando cansado, quero ir.

✍️ My English Corrections

These are examples of the kind of correction we will keep adding. The goal is natural grammar and sentence structure — not obsessing over capitalization or tiny stylistic details.

Original: I have 3 months in Canada to study english and make my english better to immigration or work for other countries.

Natural: I have three months in Canada to study English and improve my English so I can immigrate or work in other countries.
“improve my English” is more natural than “make my English better”; “immigrate” is the verb; “work in other countries” fits this meaning.
Original: Oops, I canceled without wanted.

Natural: Oops, I canceled it by accident.
After “without,” use -ing: “without wanting to.” “By accident” is the most natural everyday expression here.
Original: docs: update all the documentation from cs hub plataform

Natural: docs: update all CS Hub platform documentation
“documentation for” or “CS Hub platform documentation” is more natural; “documentation” is usually uncountable; platform spelling.
Original: adding management dashboard with ranking graph on health score section

Natural: Adding a Management Dashboard with a ranking graph to the Health Score section.
Singular count nouns need articles: “a Management Dashboard,” “a ranking graph.” We add something “to” a section.
Technical request — natural version:
In the Management Dashboard on the Health Score page, I need to add more graphs, with AM-based data visibility and query parameters. The queries will be executed through Astrobox, so the access control and query parameter handling should be centralized in a separate module. Maybe we can create an Astrobox module in /Users/bruno.faria/Documents/GitHub/api-cs-navigator. This is where my API is located. Then, in the Health Score module, we would only call the Astrobox function and pass the required parameters. Can you send me a plan for how to implement this?
Useful architecture pattern: “Module A handles X. Module B only calls A and passes Y.”
Technical documentation request — natural version:
I need to update all the documentation in this repo: /Users/bruno.faria/Documents/GitHub/cs-messaging-hub. Can you please review the entire project and update all documentation based on the current features and actual implementations? Please make sure the documentation reflects the current codebase, architecture, configuration, workflows, APIs, and infrastructure, and remove or update anything that is outdated.

More corrections from our conversations

Original: Adding one more team in the yaml for configure monitoring in the project
Natural: Adding one more team to the YAML to configure monitoring for the project.
Use “add X to Y”; “to configure” expresses purpose.
Original: feat: adding monitoring in the production yaml
Natural: feat: add monitoring to the production YAML
Conventional commits normally use the imperative: “add”.
Original: they want us to add sentry to the project
Natural: They want us to add Sentry to the project.
Pattern: want + person + to + verb.
Original: I`ll need other approve, I let you know
Natural: I’ll need someone else to approve it, but I’ll let you know.
“someone else to approve it” is natural when another person must approve.
Original: lets see if the tests is gonna pass
Natural: Let’s see if the tests pass.
“tests” is plural → “pass”. “Going to pass” is also correct; “gonna” is casual.
Original: 2. yes production have been deployed 1. i didnt understand your question
Natural: Yes, it has been deployed to production. / I didn’t understand your question.
“deployed to production”; present perfect = has been + past participle; did not + base verb.
Original: I’m gonna run the staging pipeline and I’ll tell you when it’s ready and you can go to the PO and check if it’s ok.
Natural: I’m gonna run the staging pipeline, and I’ll let you know when it’s ready. Then you can go to the POD and check if everything looks okay.
“let you know” is very natural for “te aviso”.
Original: What do you mean about this GDV?
Natural: What did you understand from this GDV?
Use “What do you mean by...?” for “o que você quer dizer com...?”; use “What did you understand from...?” for “o que você entendeu de...?”

Natural: I have three months in Canada to study English and improve my English...
“improve my English” is more natural than “make my English better”.
Original: Oops, I canceled without wanted.

“by accident” is the natural everyday expression here.

🎤 Speaking Practice

Because coding and prompting don't always force me to speak, I need deliberate speaking practice.

  1. Pick something I did today.
  2. Speak for 2 minutes without stopping.
  3. Explain a work task in English.
  4. Describe what happened in a real interaction.
  5. Write down 1–3 words/phrases I couldn't express.
  6. Bring them here and ask for correction.
Challenge: Explain your workday using at least one Present Perfect, one Present Perfect Continuous, one phrasal verb, and one modal.
Example: “I've finished my main task, but I've been working on a new feature all morning. I still need to figure out one problem. I might ask a coworker for help.”
